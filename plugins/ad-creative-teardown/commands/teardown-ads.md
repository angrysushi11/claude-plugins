Load the `ad-creative-teardown:ad-creative-teardown` skill unless it is already loaded, then run its competitor ad teardown on:

$ARGUMENTS

Use any ads, links and product details already in this conversation too. The essentials:

1. Read each ad from what the user gave: screenshots, video frames, transcripts, ad copy, or the visible text of a public Meta Ad Library page they linked. With web access, read only that page; don't download, scrape or open other pages.
2. For each ad, note the hook (the first 1–3 seconds or first line, quoted), the angle (pain, desire, proof, comparison or identity), format, offer and call to action, length, and how long it has run, with versions. Write "not visible" for anything you can't see. Never invent dates, numbers or results.
3. Rank by run time. An ad running for weeks or months, often in several versions, is probably profitable. A brand-new ad is a test, not a winner.
4. Deliver the ads table, longest-running first; what the long-running ads have in common, with ad numbers; the white space nobody uses; and five concepts for the user's product. Each concept gets a hook line of about seven spoken words, an angle, a format, and the first three seconds shot by shot with one action per shot.
5. End with how to judge the test, in two rules. A concept must beat the target CPA with headroom in the test, because CPA rises once you scale. Judge it only after enough days and events, because payments arrive over several days, not all on day one. Add no day counts, event counts or percentages of your own; apply the rules to the user's numbers if they give them.

Ask for at most one missing input. With no ads, ask for screenshots, transcripts, ad copy or an Ad Library link, and stop. With no product details, deliver the ads table, what the long-running ads share and the white space, then ask for one line about the product to write the concepts.
